# Graze: the idea and how it got here

This is the project's memory: what Graze is for, the decisions that shaped it and why, and what is still open. AGENTS.md covers how to work on it.

## The idea

Most addictive apps spend people's attention and give nothing back. Graze aims to be just as hard to put down, and to leave every player knowing more each day: languages, sciences, medicine, logic, philosophy, scripture and the great books.

The core bet is that the mechanics behind compulsive play can drive the practices that build lasting memory:

- variable rewards
- streaks
- near misses
- combos
- a vertical feed that never ends

The practices they drive are retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving, saying words aloud, and reading with comprehension checks.

Learning is not bolted onto Graze. Answering is the game: time slows at a gate and you steer into the right answer, and a right answer revives you when you crash.

## What we will and won't claim

The original brief asked for something that raises IQ, memory, reading speed and self-discipline.

**What Graze does:** it uses what the research supports.

| Practice | Source |
|---|---|
| Retrieval practice and spacing | Dunlosky et al., 2013 |
| Vocabulary and word roots | |
| Reading with comprehension rather than speed | Rayner et al., 2016 |
| If-then cues for a daily habit | |
| The production effect of saying words aloud | MacLeod et al., 2010 |
| Learning built into the core mechanic | Habgood & Ainsworth, 2011 |

**What Graze will not do:** claim IQ gains. Brain-training transfer does not hold up (Simons et al., 2016). Graze promises that you will remember what you practice, and says so on the site.

## Decisions and why

- **One file, no runtime dependencies.** The same build runs as an installable offline web app on GitHub Pages and as a single-file Claude artifact. Everything is inline, including the diagrams, maps and flags.
- **Saves merge, never overwrite.** Progress lives on the device. Transfer codes, backup files and (inside Claude) a cloud save and encrypted link codes all merge with CRDT-style rules, so two devices can't clobber each other. True peer-to-peer sync was impossible inside artifacts, so sync is relayed but end-to-end encrypted.
- **Updates never interrupt a run.** A new version waits for the player to tap Update in the menu, and its notes show once.
- **The player chooses what to learn and in what order.** The first course is the focus. Pace (new items a day) is the player's choice, because spacing only works when it is paced.
- **Courses come from free, reputable sources, and every course has been reviewed item by item.** Writers worked from OpenStax, LibreTexts, COERLL, DCC, Perseus, MedlinePlus, SEP and Gutenberg, and independent reviewers checked 28 of the courses. The most common error found was a distractor that was really a second correct answer.
- **Public-domain texts are quoted exactly; copyrighted ones are not excerpted.** *Sapiens* was requested and left out for that reason.
- **Mental math is generated by code**, so every answer is computed and the drills never run out.
- **Pictures are drawn, not downloaded.** Diagrams are hand-built SVG with highlightable parts. Maps come from Natural Earth and the US Census; flags come from flag-icons.
- **Speech is used where browsers support it.** Text-to-speech covers every language. Recognition (Chrome, Edge and Safari) checks pronunciation, and other browsers fall back to recording yourself and comparing. Latin uses an Italian voice and Greek a Modern Greek voice, since there are no ancient voices, and the game says so.
- **The visual identity is Martyr**, Donovan Baney's personal brand: Martyr Night (oxblood, brass and cream) in the game, Martyr Day on the landing page.

## Requests that shaped the Library (5.0)

The owner asked for:

- powerful, accurate, reputable learning;
- free resources such as LibreTexts;
- images and diagram quizzes;
- a choice of what to learn first;
- whole languages, with the microphone for pronunciation;
- mental math;
- a game designed to replace scrolling.

He also named subjects: world geography, philosophy, excerpts from the greatest works, the Bible, Latin, Greek, chemistry, organic chemistry, engineering, logic and fallacies, critical thinking and argument, Socrates, Plutarch and Aristotle, war and strategy, martial arts, research, grammar, writing and style, biology, medicine, software engineering, and arguments for God. Each became a course or part of one.

## Open ideas

- **Voice gates in runs:** say the word to pass a gate, for courses with speech recognition.
- **New courses:** Modern Greek; more languages (Russian, Arabic, Korean); more diagrams (skeleton, muscles, digestive tract, DNA).
- **Wider Feed formats:** short-answer typing for languages, and listening cards (hear it, pick the meaning).
- **Error reporting:** a "report a mistake" button on cards that files an issue or records the card ID.
- **Hosting:** switch GitHub Pages to the "GitHub Actions" source, so pushes stop running the extra branch build.
- **Measuring learning:** offer an optional before-and-after check on a held-out word set, so players can see the effect for themselves.
