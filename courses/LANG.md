# Language course outline (all languages)

Goal: a real beginner-to-A2 course (CEFR). A learner who finishes it should read simple texts, handle everyday conversations, and know roughly the 600 to 800 most useful words and the core grammar. Follow the outline, adapting it to the language (for example, cases for Polish, German and Latin; kana for Japanese; tones for Mandarin).

About 20 units of 25 to 35 items each. Mix in every unit:
- words (nouns with their article or gender marker where the language has one, such as "der Hund", "la casa", "dom (m)"),
- useful phrases and short sentences,
- 3 to 6 grammar items as cloze cues, such as `Yo ___ español. (hablar)` with answer `hablo` and distractors like `habla`, `hablas`.

Suggested units:
1. Sounds, greetings and courtesy
2. People, pronouns and "to be"
3. Numbers and age
4. Family
5. Food and drink
6. Days, months and telling time
7. Colors, describing things, and adjective agreement
8. Everyday verbs in the present tense
9. Home and household
10. Places in town and directions
11. Travel and transport
12. Body and health
13. Clothing, shopping and prices
14. Work and school
15. Weather, nature and seasons
16. Question words, connectors and frequency words
17. The past tense, core forms
18. Future and plans, wanting and being able
19. Feelings and opinions
20. Essential conversation sentences

Rules for language items:
- `c` is the target language; `a` is the English meaning. Give the most common meaning; keep `a` short.
- Use the standard modern form of the language (Latin: classical spelling with macrons optional, but be consistent). Use correct accents and diacritics everywhere.
- Prefer high-frequency vocabulary. Every word should be one a learner meets constantly.
- Add `ex` and `exT` to at least a third of the items.
- Readings: about every third unit, in the target language, 50 to 160 words, using taught vocabulary.
- Cue uniqueness: if the same word appears twice in different senses, make the cues distinct, for example by adding context in parentheses.
